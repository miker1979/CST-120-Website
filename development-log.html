September 3, 2026 · Ghostline Software / RailLogic

Southwest service audit locks and Northwest corridor validation begins

RailLogic completed its Southwest route-and-service audit and immediately moved into Northwest validation. The work focused on making the physical guideway, passenger-station catalog, service patterns, TrainRun schedules, and reservation screen agree with one another before each corridor is locked.

  • Cleaned and consolidated RailNodeData, removed duplicate California node definitions, and added the missing Los Banos, Modesto, and Stockton passenger nodes required by the two California trunk alignments.
  • Added matching passenger stations and updated the Central Valley physical path so the CA-99 side now runs Fresno → Modesto → Stockton → Sacramento.
  • Built the western California Bay approach from Los Angeles → Burbank Airport → Santa Clarita → Los Banos → San Jose and linked it to the existing San Jose → San Francisco → Sacramento infrastructure.
  • Locked the California Central Valley Direct as Los Angeles → Burbank → Bakersfield → Fresno → Stockton → Sacramento and the Regional as Los Angeles → Burbank → Santa Clarita → Bakersfield → Fresno → Modesto → Stockton → Sacramento.
  • Validated the Central Valley service in both directions at 392 miles: Direct runs in 1 hr 58 min at a $86 fare, while Regional runs in 2 hr 17 min at a $74 fare.
  • Extended the Bay service into a full California trunk. The California Bay Direct now runs Los Angeles → Burbank → Los Banos → San Jose → San Francisco → Sacramento, while the California Bay Regional adds Santa Clarita and continues through Sacramento to Redding.
  • Found and corrected a reservation-data dependency: bookable stations are generated from scheduled passenger stops on actual TrainRuns. Los Banos, San Jose, and San Francisco did not appear until the new Bay Direct and Regional services received real repeating departures.
  • Validated Los Angeles ↔ San Francisco in both directions at 408 miles: Direct runs in 1 hr 51 min for $90, while Regional runs in 2 hr 1 min for $77.
  • With the California work complete, marked the Southwest Region complete and locked and began the Northwest audit without changing the already validated Bay service again.
  • Locked NW-01 Northern Rockies Direct: Seattle → Spokane → Butte → Billings → Sheridan → Cheyenne. The 1,280-mile Direct runs in 4 hr 49 min for $282 versus the National Nonstop at 4 hr 10 min for $320, producing a clear service-tier tradeoff.
  • Locked NW-02 Pacific Northwest Spine Direct: Seattle → Portland → Redding → Sacramento. Direct covers 755 miles in 2 hr 48 min for $166, while the 758-mile Regional takes 3 hr 48 min for $144. The Direct saves a full hour for a $22 premium.
  • NW-03 is already represented by the California Bay service completed during the Southwest pass, so the next untouched Northwest Direct is NW-04 Portland ↔ Boise.

Result: Southwest is no longer an open-ended design pass; it is a tested, bidirectional passenger-service region with California’s Central Valley and Bay trunks operating through the same schedule and reservation model as the rest of RailLogic. Northwest validation has begun with its first two untouched Direct corridors already locked.

Next: resume with NW-04 Portland ↔ Boise, compare the Columbia–Idaho Direct against the Columbia–Snake Regional, continue the Northwest route-by-route audit, and defer fleet-size reconciliation until the full service catalog has been validated.

September 2, 2026 · Ghostline Software / RailLogic

Reservation engine, multi-transfer routing, and Southwest service audit reach a new operating milestone

RailLogic made a major jump from route-catalog construction into a more complete passenger operating model. Recent work unified service timing, fare calculation, multi-train pathfinding, transfer presentation, and route auditing while the rail product name was finalized as RailLogic.

  • Standardized the commercial maximum service speed at 311 mph (500 km/h) and added acceleration / cruise / deceleration timing so short segments no longer assume instant travel at maximum speed.
  • Locked mileage-based passenger fares at $0.19 per mile for Regional, $0.22 per mile for Direct, and $0.25 per mile for National Hub Nonstop, with mixed-service trips priced from the actual mileage and service type of each leg rather than a transfer surcharge.
  • Added unlimited multi-transfer rail pathfinding that can combine Regional, Direct, and National Nonstop services, uses real scheduled TrainRuns, respects minimum connection windows, handles time zones, and ranks viable itineraries by arrival time and transfer count.
  • Improved connection ranking so repeated schedule choices on the same route combination are reduced to the best useful options instead of flooding the passenger with near-duplicate itineraries.
  • Reworked the reservation presentation into a wide planning column and a continuous right-side timetable showing Station, Arrival, Departure, and Service / Action, with BOARD, TRANSFER, and EXIT behavior kept inside one readable journey table.
  • Refined connection cards so each itinerary clearly exposes fare, departure / arrival timing, total duration, transfer count, service mix, and transfer cities before the passenger selects a departure.
  • Expanded Southwest Direct service data, corrected time-zone and rail-node behavior, and continued aligning the reservation screen with the same service and schedule model used by the route catalog.
  • Added the Western Intermountain Regional passenger service from Phoenix to Salt Lake City, serving Camp Verde, Flagstaff, Kingman, Henderson, Las Vegas, St. George, Cedar City, and Provo before reaching Salt Lake City.
  • Audited and validated Phoenix ↔ Salt Lake City National Nonstop service at 828 miles and Phoenix ↔ Seattle National Nonstop service at 1,679 miles in both directions, including fares, elapsed time, daily departures, average speed, and time-zone behavior. The Phoenix ↔ Salt Lake City Direct overlay was also validated in both directions.
  • Locked a new network-design rule for the Regional audit: a Regional service should normally connect meaningful major terminals, Direct stations, National Hubs, or inter-regional gateways along the same physical corridor rather than terminate at an arbitrary Regional station.
  • Established a common national timetable pattern for implementation across all six regions: Nonstop → Direct → Regional → Direct → Regional → Direct → Nonstop between the four-hour National Nonstop anchors. This operating pattern is a planning standard and will be wired into the timetable generator next.

Result: RailLogic now behaves much more like an integrated passenger railway model rather than separate route, booking, and timetable demonstrations. Physical corridors, passenger services, real departures, fares, transfers, and the reservation UI are converging on one operating system.

Next: implement the national departure pattern in the timetable generator, continue the Southwest audit through Regional and Direct endpoint structure, rework corridors such as the Rio Grande Regional where an ordinary station currently acts as an artificial terminus, and keep validating every service in both directions before locking it.

August 28, 2026 · Ghostline Software / RailLogic

Reservation workflow moves onto the service-based rail network

RailLogic moved another step from network design into a passenger-facing operating prototype. The reservation interface is now being tied directly to the same service, timetable, and route data used by Route Explorer instead of treating booking as a separate demonstration layer.

  • Expanded the National Hub route catalog through the Bismarck pairing set, adding long-distance combinations including Bismarck-to-New-York, Pittsburgh, Nashville, and Miami while continuing to use shared physical corridor segments rather than duplicating track logic.
  • Refined several National Hub alignments so routes follow the preferred operating geography instead of simply passing through another National Hub when a cleaner bypass is available.
  • Updated the passenger journey wrapper so National Hub and Regional journeys can point directly at a RailService; ordinary-station trips temporarily retain the legacy direct / one-transfer highway-route planner while the full service conversion continues.
  • Connected the reservation screen to selected departures and train inventory so it can display the train name and live available-seat count for the chosen schedule.
  • Moved seat selection into a dedicated, scrollable Choose a seat window with a four-column seat layout, disabled unavailable seats, highlighted current selection, and return-to-reservation behavior.
  • Reworked the main reservation results area around readable journey details instead of a permanently visible seat grid, freeing the workspace for route, schedule, transfer, and trip information.
  • Added a Copy trip action so the currently displayed itinerary can be copied directly from the reservation screen.
  • Improved transfer handling so the selected itinerary can show the transfer station and calculated connection time between arrival and the next departure.
  • Kept booking controls for passenger information, departure selection, payment method, fare display, reservation, and cancellation while integrating the newer journey-detail workflow.

Result: the reservation system is beginning to operate as a true front end for the RailLogic network model. Route Explorer, scheduled services, transfer timing, train capacity, and seat selection are converging on the same underlying data instead of being maintained as separate demonstrations.

Next: finish the remaining National Hub route pairs, continue replacing the legacy ordinary-station planner with service-based Regional journeys, validate transfer windows across longer itineraries, and keep tightening the reservation UI as the operating model becomes more complete.

August 26, 2026 · Ghostline Software / RailLogic

National Hub route build moves into a structured 67-route network pass

RailLogic has moved from designing individual showcase corridors into a repeatable National Hub route-build process. The current pass applies the same physical-corridor, station, service, and scheduling rules across the full hub network instead of forcing every pair into an immediate route.

  • Advanced the current National Hub route-build sequence to roughly 22 of 67 planned routes, with each route checked against available guideway, passenger geography, and practical transfer structure.
  • Revised the active National Hub set so Albuquerque, New Mexico replaces Denver, Colorado; Albuquerque is now used for the remaining National Hub route generation unless the network plan is revised again.
  • Locked the rule that alternate physical corridors serving meaningfully different geography are regular scheduled routes, not hidden contingency-only paths. The default alternate-corridor pattern is two trains per day in each direction—one morning and one evening—with additional service during holiday and high-demand periods.
  • Prioritized routes that can be built from the guideway already represented by the Interstate-system corridor catalog. When the better primary path depends on a not-yet-built U.S.-highway corridor, that hub pair is deferred rather than routed through an unrealistic Interstate detour.
  • Applied that rule to Cheyenne ↔ Houston, which remains deferred until the planned US-287 corridor is added during the corridor-expansion phase.
  • Locked universal trip-planning behavior so reservation searches will eventually return all valid scheduled itineraries between approved passenger stations, including feeder or Regional legs into gateway stations, direct services, non-stop services, and valid transfer combinations.
  • Standardized the passenger operating window around stations opening at 6:00 AM, first departures at 7:00 AM, Regional departures repeating every two hours, and no train departures after midnight; trains may still arrive after midnight when the booked itinerary requires it.
  • Refined the guideway geometry rule: 375-mph mainline alignments should remain as straight and flat as practical with very large-radius curves, while tighter curves are permitted where necessary if trains slow before entering them. Very low-speed curves around 40–50 mph are reserved for terminals, junctions, urban constraints, and other special-use locations.

Result: route generation is now governed by a consistent network-wide operating model instead of one-off corridor decisions. That makes the remaining hub build more realistic, easier to audit, and better prepared for later timetable and reservation-system integration.

Next: complete the remaining National Hub routes that can use existing Interstate guideway, build the deferred U.S.-highway corridor expansions, return to deferred hub pairs such as Cheyenne–Houston, and connect the completed route catalog more deeply to scheduled journey planning.

Transcontinental hub routing and congestion-aware route selection expand

RailLogic continued its move from a route-reference model into a scalable operating network by expanding permanent hub-to-hub travel routes, locking additional transcontinental corridor data, and adding logic for connected trips and alternate operating paths.

  • Added TR-508 — Northern Tier Corridor, a direct Seattle-to-New-York transcontinental route using the I-90 spine through the Northwest, northern Plains, upper Midwest, Chicago, and the eastern approach into New York.
  • Expanded the permanent route register with TR-506 Pioneer-Atlantic Line from Salt Lake City to New York, TR-507 Gem State Seaboard Line from Boise to New York, TR-509 Piedmont-Seaboard Express from Atlanta to New York, TR-510 Sunshine-Empire Express from Miami to New York, TR-511 Wasatch & Sound Express from Salt Lake City to Seattle, and TR-512 Intermountain-Texan Line from Salt Lake City to Dallas-Fort Worth.

Result: RailLogic can now represent a much larger transcontinental hub network while separating passenger service, physical travel routes, transfer planning, and real-time operating-route choice into independent layers.

August 21, 2026 · Ghostline Software / RailLogic

Service-based rail operations, route topology, and real schedule timing established

RailLogic advanced from a highway-and-station reference model into the beginning of an operational rail network. The work now separates the physical route a train follows from the passenger service pattern and the individual scheduled train that operates it.

  • Established the 12 National Hubs across the six planned rail regions, including station capacity, storage-yard, maintenance-facility, and local-time-zone data.
  • Added the first permanent multi-corridor travel route: TR-101 — Intermountain Meridian, connecting Phoenix and Salt Lake City through I-17, I-40, I-11 / US-93, the Las Vegas connector, and I-15.
  • Separated passenger-serving stations from track-only guideway junctions so the network can model real routing and mileage without exposing technical connectors in passenger searches.
  • Created the Desert-Salt Arrow National Hub Nonstop Express and the Intermountain Meridian Regional service pattern on the same physical route.
  • Defined both northbound and southbound service directions, with the Regional service scheduling every passenger station and 15-minute intermediate dwell times.
  • Added individual daily train runs for the Desert-Salt Arrow at 7:00 AM and 7:00 PM from each terminal, separating a service pattern from the train departures that actually operate it.
  • Added time-zone-aware arrival calculations so Phoenix-to-Salt-Lake-City schedules can correctly account for Arizona's year-round time standard and Utah daylight-saving changes.
  • Expanded Route Explorer into an operational route inspector that can display service type, direction, corridor path, route nodes, scheduled passenger stops, mileage, travel duration, daily departures, and estimated arrivals.

Result: RailLogic now has a scalable operational foundation for named passenger services, route-specific schedules, reversible travel directions, later capacity simulation, and transfer-wait calculations.

Next: add scheduled Regional train runs, build the next National Hub-to-Hub travel routes, and begin the service-based Journey Planner that can compare direct, connecting, and capacity-limited options.

August 19, 2026 · Ghostline Software / TransitLogic

TransitLogic Phase 2 smart matching and HOS-aware trip planning begins

With the Phase 1 carrier workflow frozen, TransitLogic moved into its decision-intelligence phase. The new work is focused on helping a carrier understand not only which loads are available, but which loads best fit the truck, operating costs, schedule, and next move.

  • Added a 0–100% explainable Load Match Score using equipment preference, deadhead, True Profit per mile, profit per hour, schedule cushion, and all-mile rate.
  • Added Best Match sorting and a Best Matches quick filter so stronger opportunities can be surfaced without manually comparing every row.
  • Added a Why This Load explanation inside Load Details so recommendation scores remain transparent.
  • Added dedicated pickup and delivery date/time fields, transit-window information, and a simplified larger-font load-board layout for faster scanning.
  • Added left-side red, yellow, and green urgency indicators based on the available pickup-to-delivery window, plus a clear Details action for deeper load review.
  • Added a Next Load Trip Planner that searches compatible outbound freight after the selected load delivers and compares two-load chains.
  • Added combined trip metrics including gross revenue, True Profit, operating miles, layover time, profit per hour, and chain score.
  • Added HOS-aware schedule-feasibility planning using configurable remaining driving hours, cycle hours, stop service time, estimated break/rest requirements, and final-delivery cushion.
  • Trip chains are now classified as HOS Feasible, HOS Tight, or Not Feasible, with the feasibility result incorporated into the chain score.
  • HOS calculations are presented as planning assistance rather than guaranteed legal compliance; the prototype does not guess actual ELD history, exemptions, or sleeper-berth exceptions.

Result: TransitLogic is beginning to move beyond a conventional freight listing board toward a carrier decision platform that can explain which load fits, what it is expected to earn, what should follow it, and whether the combined schedule appears realistically workable.
