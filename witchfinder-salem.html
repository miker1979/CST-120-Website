The Witchfinder: Salem

A single-player historical mystery-horror investigation set during the Salem witch trials of 1692.

Called by the magistrate and clergy after unexplained deaths, possessions, and accusations, you must decide what is true before fear turns Salem against itself.

Single PlayerHistorical MysterySupernatural HorrorUnity
Current Concept

The town demands judgment. The evidence demands patience.

Every case begins with an incident: a death, a possession, a stolen relic, or an accusation that threatens to tear the town apart.

You investigate scenes, interview witnesses, follow suspects, secure warrants, and connect evidence before bringing anyone to trial. Most accusations are not proof. Some people are hiding shame, fear, grudges, or crimes of their own. Ten real witches are hidden within Salem.

Investigation Loop

Search
Examine scenes, homes, documents, and recovered objects for evidence that can survive scrutiny.
Question
Challenge witnesses, compare testimony, and determine whether fear is shaping the story.
Follow
Surveil suspects to uncover secret meetings, concealed motives, and supernatural activity without being seen.
Accuse
Present a complete case at trial—or risk condemning an innocent person while the true coven remains free.

Current Direction

The truth is buried beneath hysteria.

The Witchfinder: Salem uses the real fear and injustice of the era as the pressure around a fictional supernatural investigation—not as an excuse to treat every accusation as true.

A town in panic

Afflicted girls, clergy, neighbors, and officials can offer sincere, mistaken, self-serving, or manipulated testimony.

A careful investigator

Build cases from corroborated evidence. A thorough investigation can expose a witch; a reckless one can destroy a life.

A hidden covenant

Ten real witches are working toward a larger ritual. Each success or failure changes what remains possible in Salem.

Fictional historical horror: The supernatural threat is invented. The investigation is built around the consequences of fear, pressure, and accusations in 1692 Salem.

Practical software, original interactive entertainment, and a disciplined path from prototype to product.
